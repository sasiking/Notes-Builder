UPSC / APPSC ANCIENT INDIAN HISTORY BLUEPRINT

Indus Valley Civilization (c. 2500 – 1750 BCE)

Harappan Civilization • Bronze Age • Proto-Historic Urban Revolution
I. Discovery, Geographical Reach & Evolution

1. Discovery Milestones & Nomenclature

  • Proto-Historic Civilization: Script existed but remains undeciphered (boustrophedon / pictographic).
  • Key Archaeological Pioneers:
    ▫ 1826: Charles Masson visited Harappa and first reported the mounds.
    ▫ 1861 & 1873: Alexander Cunningham founded ASI (1861) and collected early seals.
    ▫ 1904: Lord Curzon established the formal Archaeological Department.
    ▫ 1921: Sir John Marshall (DG-ASI) announced discovery; Daya Ram Sahni excavated Harappa.
    ▫ 1922: R.D. Banerji excavated Mohenjodaro ("Mound of the Dead") in Larkana, Sindh.
  • Nomenclature: Named *Harappan Civilization* after its type-site. Rigveda refers to it as Hariyupiya.
  • Radiocarbon Dating: D.P. Agrawal dates mature phase to c. 2300–2000 BCE.

3. The Four Evolutionary Stages

  • Pre-Harappan Stage: Formative agro-pastoral village settlements (e.g., Mehrgarh, Kili Gul Mohammad, Quetta, Nal).
  • Early / Proto-Harappan Stage: Emergence of proto-urban characteristics, fort walls, and early crafts (e.g., Kalibangan I, Amri, Kot Diji).
  • Mature Harappan Stage: Fully developed metropolitan urban centers (Harappa, Mohenjodaro, Lothal, Dholavira).
  • Late / Post-Harappan Stage: Urban de-densification, ruralization, and localization of material culture.

2. Geographical Extent (~13,00,000 sq. km)

  • Total Area: Over 1.3 million sq. km, forming a vast triangle.
  • Geographical Boundary Matrix:
    ▫ North: Manda (Chenab river, Jammu & Kashmir).
    ▫ South: Daimabad (Pravara river, Maharashtra).
    ▫ West: Sutkagan Dor (Dasht river, Makran coast, Pak-Iran border).
    ▫ East: Alamgirpur (Hindon river, Meerut, UP).
  • Demographics & Racial Diversity: Total population ~2 lakhs (Mohenjodaro alone ~35,000–40,000). Multi-racial synthesis comprising Mediterranean (dominant), Proto-Australoid, Mongoloid, and Alpinoid races.
📌 Contemporary Trade Lands in Mesopotamian Texts:
• Meluhha: Land of Indus (major exporter of Sindon/cotton).
• Dilmun: Intermediate clearing station (modern Bahrain).
• Magan: Contemporary coastal Oman.

III. Urban Town Planning, Sanitation & Architecture

1. The Gridiron Layout & Zonation

  • Grid Pattern ("Chessboard Pattern"): Streets were straight, wide, running North-South and East-West, cutting each other at precise $90^\circ$ right angles.
  • Dual Citadel-Lower Town Zonation:
    ▫ Western Citadel (Acropolis): Raised on mud-brick platforms; fortified; housed public buildings, granaries, and the ruling aristocracy.
    ▫ Eastern Lower City: Larger, non-citadel grid for housing common citizens, artisans, and merchant classes.
    ▫ Exception: Dholavira is divided into 3 tiers (Citadel, Middle Town, Lower Town); Chanhudaro has no citadel.
  • Brick Dimensions: Standardized ratio of $1 : 2 : 4$ (Thickness : Breadth : Length) observed across every site regardless of distance!
  • Orientation: Gates positioned North and South; streets provided with street lamp posts at ends.

2. Underground Drainage & Domestic Privacy

  • Underground Covered Drainage: Drains ran beneath streets covered with limestone slabs or kiln-burnt tiles. Provided with manholes/inspection sumps at regular intervals for de-silting.
  • Domestic Hygiene: Bathrooms floored with burnt tiles; kitchen waste drained into street mains through terracotta wall chutes.
  • Residential Privacy Architecture:
    ▫ Outer house walls lacked street-level windows; residential courtyards remained shielded from direct exterior view.
    ▫ Doors opened into side alleys rather than main thoroughfares (except at Lothal).
    ▫ Street lamps were mounted at street corners for night lighting.
    ▫ Wells and backyards for cattle maintenance in houses.
  • CRITICAL NEGATIVE LIST: What the Indus People Did NOT Know:
    ▫ Metals: Did NOT know Iron (IVC was a Bronze Age culture using copper-tin/arsenic alloys; Iron appeared in the Gangetic plains c. 1000 BCE).
    ▫ Animals: Did NOT know the Lion (surrounded by tigers, elephants, rhinos, humped bulls; lion is not depicted on seals). Horse knowledge remains marginal and disputed.
    ▫ Crops: No direct evidence for cultivation of Sugarcane and Potatoes. Primary staple was wheat and barley.
    ▫ Structures: NO Temples or standing monumental palaces have ever been found.
IV. Agrarian Economy, Trade Routes & Craft Specialization

1. Agrarian Foundation

  • Crops Grown: Wheat (two varieties), Barley (primary staples), Mustard, Sesamum, Dates, Peas, and Millets.
  • Cotton Production: First civilization in the ancient world to domesticate and weave cotton (proven at Mehrgarh). Greeks called it Sindon (derived from Sindh).
  • Irrigation: Largely based on annual inundation of fertile river alluvium; canals known at Shortughai (Afghanistan) and stone water reservoirs at Dholavira. Gabarbands (stone dams) used in Baluchistan.

2. Craft Specialization

  • Bead-Making Industry: Highly advanced workshops at Chanhudaro and Lothal using carnelian, agate, lapis lazuli, and steatite.
  • Metallurgy: Mastered the Cire-Perdue (lost-wax technique) for bronze casting (e.g., Mohenjodaro Dancing Girl).
  • Pottery: Sturdy red pottery painted with black geometric and animal motifs (Black-on-Red ware / RPW / BPW).
  • Carpentry & Shipbuilding: Dockyard operations at Lothal and wooden carts.

3. Domestic & External Trade Networks

  • Mechanism: Barter system aided by standardized cubical chert weights (binary ratio: 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64; decimal: 160, 320, 640).
  • Provenance of Imported Raw Materials:
    ▫ Copper: Khetri Mines (Rajasthan) & Oman.
    ▫ Gold: Kolar gold fields (South India) and Mesopotamia.
    ▫ Silver & Tin: Afghanistan and Iran.
    ▫ Lapis Lazuli: Badakhshan (Afghanistan).
    ▫ Steatite: Narmada Valley and Gujarat.
    ▫ Jade: Central Asia.
    ▫ Turquoise: Khorasan (Iran).
  • Archaeological Trade Evidence: Indus seals found at Kish, Ur, Lagash, and Tell Asmar (Mesopotamia); Persian Gulf button seal found at Lothal. Cuneiform texts identify Meluhha as a trading partner.
V. Social Conditions, Religious Beliefs & Polity

1. Social Stratification & Women

  • Class Stratification: Differentiation between palace/citadel elite and workmen quarters in lower city points to marked socio-economic hierarchy (class-based society, not caste).
  • Matriarchal Tendencies: Abundant terracotta female figurines and Mother Goddess seals point to a high social status for women and matriarchal reverence.
  • Life & Leisure: Vegetarian and non-vegetarian food; dressed in cotton and wool; enjoyed board games, dice, chess, and bull-fighting.

2. Nature of Polity (Who Ruled IVC?)

  • Lack of Monarchy: Total absence of dynastic war imagery, monuments to kings, or royal tombs.
  • Historiographical Interpretations:
    ▫ R.S. Sharma: Ruled by a Merchant Class Oligarchy (urban mercantile focus, standardized weights, contract enforcement).
    ▫ D.D. Kosambi: Ruled by a Priestly Aristocracy (similar to Sumerian temple-states, citing Great Bath and Priest-King statue).
    ▫ Modern View: Municipal city councils or localized administrative oligarchies ensuring civic standardization.

3. Religious Beliefs & Practices

  • No Public Temples: Religion was domestic and personal.
  • Mother Goddess (Matridevi): Chief female deity symbolizing fertility and vegetation (seal showing plant sprouting from woman’s womb).
  • Pashupati Mahadev (Proto-Shiva): Three-horned yogic deity surrounded by fauna (elephant, tiger, rhino, buffalo, and 2 deer).
  • Nature & Animal Worship: Pipal tree (*Ficus religiosa*), humped bull, unicorns, snakes, pigeons.
  • Phallic Cults: Widespread stone *lingas* and *yonis* (fertility cults).
  • Amulets & Superstition: Large-scale terracotta amulets to ward off malevolent evil spirits.
VI. Script Enigma & Theories of Collapse

The Indus Script

  • Characteristics: Pictographic and logographic; written in Boustrophedon style (right-to-left in first line, left-to-right in second line).
  • Corpus: ~400 distinct signs found mainly on steatite seals, copper tablets, ivory sticks, and pottery. Over 1,500 seals excavated.
  • Linguistic Affiliation Debates:
    ▫ Iravatham Mahadevan & Asko Parpola: Affinity with the Proto-Dravidian language.
    ▫ S.R. Rao: Affinity with archaic Indo-European / Sanskrit phonetics.

Theories on the Decline of IVC

  • 1. Sudden Catastrophe / Aryan Invasion:
    ▫ Proposed by Mortimer Wheeler & Gordon Childe.
    ▫ Cited Rigvedic hymns praising Indra as Purandara ("destroyer of forts"), and scattered skeletons at Mohenjodaro.
    ▫ Critique: Disproven by modern forensic evidence showing no mass battle trauma.
  • 2. Environmental & Ecological Rupture (Consensus View):
    ▫ Drying up of Saraswati (Ghaggar-Hakra): H.T. Lambrick & M.R. Mughal.
    ▫ Tectonic Uplift & River Diversion: Robert Raikes & E.J.H. Mackay.
    ▫ Aridity & Desertification: Gurdip Singh (pollen analysis showing massive rainfall drop c. 2000 BCE).

VIII. Critical Archaeological Case Examples
  • Hydraulic Engineering at Dholavira: Positioned between the Mansar and Manhar seasonal streams, Dholavira engineered a series of 16 rock-cut reservoirs to survive hyper-arid Kutch conditions, showcasing advanced rainwater harvesting over 4,500 years ago.
  • Marine Navigation at Lothal: Excavated by S.R. Rao, Lothal features a trapezoidal burnt-brick basin ($214 \times 36 \text{ m}$) linked via an inlet channel to the Gulf of Khambhat tide, functioning as the world's earliest known tidal dockyard.
  • Craft Specialization at Chanhudaro: The absence of an administrative citadel paired with bead-polishers, furnaces, steatite seal-cutting workshops, and shell drills proves it operated as an industrial manufacturing hub serving external trade networks.

XI. Exam-Trick Boxes (Avoid Common Traps!)
  • TRAP 1: "The Harappans used iron weapons and tools." ➔ WRONG! IVC was purely a Bronze Age civilization. Iron was completely unknown until c. 1000 BCE in the Later Vedic era.
  • TRAP 2: "The cow was the most depicted animal on Indus seals." ➔ WRONG! The cow was never depicted on any Harappan seal! The most frequent motif is the single-horned mythical Unicorn, followed by the humped bull.
  • TRAP 3: "Every Harappan site had a fortified dual citadel." ➔ WRONG! Chanhudaro had no citadel at all, while Dholavira had a 3-tier division (Citadel, Middle Town, Lower Town).
  • TRAP 4: "Monumental temples were found at Mohenjo-daro." ➔ WRONG! No temples or public shrines were ever built in IVC. Religious practices centered on home altars, open-air icons, and the Great Bath.
  • TRAP 5: "Harappan script has been deciphered as Sanskrit." ➔ WRONG! The script remains completely undeciphered. It is logo-syllabic/pictographic, written boustrophedon.

Mains Practice • Question 1 "Nature of social conditions of Indus Valley Civilization." Discuss. (10 Marks)
View Model Answer Structuring Framework
Model Structuring Framework:
• Introduction: Define IVC as a proto-historic Bronze Age urban civilization characterized by social complexity without the formal birth-based Varna system of the later Vedic era.
• Socio-Economic Stratification (Class System): Spatial town planning proves social hierarchy. The elevated western citadel was inhabited by the ruling elite/aristocracy, while the lower town housed traders, artisans, and manual laborers. Uniform 2-room workmen barracks at Harappa indicate an organized working class.
• Status of Women & Family: The predominance of Mother Goddess terracotta figurines, nursing mothers, and fertility seals suggests a society with matriarchal reverence. Statues like the Bronze Dancing Girl reflect participation in performing arts. Women wore sophisticated jewelry (bead necklaces, bangles of faience/shell, lipsticks/ochre found at Chanhudaro).
• Diet & Lifestyle: Mixed diet of wheat, barley, mutton, fish, and beef. Leisure centered on board games, terracotta dice, and bull-leaping.
• Conclusion: Summarize that Harappan society was civic-minded, highly utilitarian, secular, and peaceful, prioritizing public sanitation and commercial welfare over monumental religious display.
Mains Practice • Question 2 "Discuss briefly about the growth of the agrarian system and economic conditions in IVC." (10 Marks)
View Model Answer Structuring Framework
Model Structuring Framework:
• Introduction: Highlight that the urban prosperity of the Indus cities rested on a resilient, high-yield rural agrarian surplus capable of supporting specialized non-food-producing urban centers.
• Agricultural Backbone: Cultivated two seasonal crops (Rabi and Kharif) across the fertile alluvium. Advanced farming evidenced by terracotta plough models at Banawali and furrowed ploughed fields at Kalibangan. First producers of cotton in the ancient world. Massive state granaries (Harappa, Mohenjodaro) stored surplus as emergency food security.
• Industrial & Secondary Production: Flourishing craft sectors: metallurgy (bronze casting via lost-wax technique), bead factories at Chanhudaro/Lothal, brick-kilns, and shipbuilding at Lothal.
• Internal & Maritime External Trade: Sophisticated trade via coastal dockyards (Lothal, Sutkagan Dor, Balakot). Extensive sea and caravan routes linked to the Persian Gulf (Dilmun) and Mesopotamia (Meluhha). Standardized cubical chert weights prevented market fraud.
• Conclusion: Connect the collapse of this delicate agrarian surplus (due to ecological desiccation of rivers) directly to the de-urbanization of the civilization.
Mains Practice • Question 3 "To what extent can the urban planning and drainage architecture of the Harappan civilization offer sustainable solutions to modern Indian urban challenges?" (15 Marks)
View Model Answer Structuring Framework
Model Structuring Framework:
• Introduction: Point out that Harappan town planning represents the "First Urbanization" of South Asia, demonstrating engineering foresight over 4,500 years ago.
• Key Ancient Solutions vs. Modern Challenges:
▫ Urban Flooding & Drainage: Harappan covered underground sewers with access inspection chambers contrast sharply with modern open, clogged urban nullahs causing waterlogging in cities like Bengaluru and Delhi.
▫ Water Scarcity & Rainwater Harvesting: Dholavira's stepwells, bunds, and rock-cut reservoirs present a sustainable blueprint for decentralized rainwater harvesting in drought-prone zones.
▫ Grid Zoning & Standardization: Clear separation between industrial/artisan zones (Chanhudaro), residential quarters, and commercial corridors prevents the unregulated mixed-use urban sprawl common today.
▫ Building Materials: Standardized, reusable kiln-burnt bricks and natural bitumen water-proofing offer natural, durable alternatives to heat-retaining concrete facades.
• Conclusion: End with a quote or perspective that modern city missions (such as AMRUT and Smart Cities Mission) can draw inspiration from Harappa's civic discipline, zero-waste drainage, and climate-resilient water engineering.
