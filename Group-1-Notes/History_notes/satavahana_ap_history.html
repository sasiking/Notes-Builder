Satavahana Empire: Comprehensive Master Notes

Origins • Political Chronology • Administration • Economy & Trade • Society • Religion, Literature & Art
I. Origin, Identity & Historical Sources

1. Literary & Epigraphic Sources

  • Puranic Nomenclature: Puranas (Matsya, Vayu, Vishnu) refer to them as Andhras, Andhra-Bhrityas, or Andhrajatiyah. Matsya Purana lists 30 kings reigning for ~450 years.
  • Inscriptional Identity: Their own epigraphs and coins consistently use the name Satavahana or Satakani, never calling themselves "Andhra".
  • Key Inscriptions:
    ▫ Naneghat Inscription: Issued by Queen Naganika (widow of Satakarni I); records early Vedic sacrifices and the first epigraphic evidence of tax-free land gifts (Brahmadeya).
    ▫ Nasik Prasasti: Composed by Gautami Balasri; eulogizes the military exploits and virtues of Gautamiputra Satakarni.
    ▫ Hathigumpha Inscription: Kharavela of Kalinga records his military engagements with Satakarni I.

2. Debate on Original Homeland & Capitals

  • Andhra Origin Theory: Advocated by Vincent Smith, K. Gopalachari; supported by ancient references like Aitareya Brahmana and Pliny's accounts of fortified Andhra settlements.
  • Kotilingala Evidence: Discovery of pre-Satavahana and early Satavahana punch-marked coins (bearing names of Gobhada, Samagopa, and Chhimuka/Simuka) at Kotilingala (Karimnagar) supports an eastern Deccan cradle.
  • Capitals over Time:
    ▫ Initial power bases at Kotilingala and Pratishthana (Paithan) on the Godavari.
    ▫ Shifted eastwards during foreign Saka pressure, making Dhanyakataka (Amaravati/Dharanikota) on the Krishna river their imperial seat.

III. Administrative Structure

1. Central & Provincial Hierarchy

  • Monarchy: Hereditary monarchy checked by Dharmic codes; kings did not claim absolute divine incarnation but assumed high moral paternalism.
  • Administrative Divisions:
    ▫ Aharas (Rashtras): Provinces governed by royal governors called Amatyas or Mahamatras.
    ▫ Nigamas: Urban market towns with municipal merchant councils (e.g., Dhanyakataka, Sopara, Kalyan).
    ▫ Grama: Basic rural unit headed by a village officer called Gramani.
  • Feudal Ranks: Three tiers of hereditary feudatories:
    1. Raja: Royal feudatory with coin-minting rights.
    2. Maharathi: Regional military lord empowered to issue land grants.
    3. Mahabhoja: Chieftain with administrative privileges.

2. Military & Revenue Administration

  • Military Camps (Katakkas & Skandhavaras): Settlements and forts serving as regional administrative headquarters during royal tours.
  • Gaulmika (Rural Police/Military Chief): In charge of a military company (Gulma: 9 chariots, 9 elephants, 25 horses, 45 foot soldiers); maintained law, order, and tax collection in rural hinterlands.
  • Taxation Structure:
    ▫ Bhaga: State's share of agrarian yield, traditionally set at 1/6th.
    ▫ Kara: Periodic crop tax levied on peasants.
    ▫ Deya & Veyya: Assessment duties and customs tolls collected at city gates and ports.
  • Introduction of Land Grants: First Indian dynasty to issue royal copper plate grants gifting tax-exempt land to Brahmins (Brahmadeya) and Buddhist monasteries (Devabhoga), introducing administrative immunities.
IV. Economy, Guilds & Maritime Trade

1. Craft Guilds (Srenis / Nikayas)

  • Autonomous Production Units: Craft workers organized into self-governing guilds called Srenis, headed by a Sreshthi or Mukhiya.
  • Major Guilds Recorded in Epigraphs:
    ▫ Kularikas: Potters
    ▫ Odayantrikas: Fabricators of water-lifting hydraulic machines
    ▫ Tilikas: Oil-pressers
    ▫ Kamarikas: Iron-smiths
    ▫ Kolikas: Weavers
    ▫ Tesakaras: Polished stone-cutters
  • Banking Functions: Guilds acted as modern financial trusts; kings, merchants, and queens deposited cash endowments (*akshayanivi*) with them to pay perpetual interest for supporting monks and lamp offerings.

2. Internal & Foreign Maritime Commerce

  • Periplus of the Erythraean Sea: Greco-Roman sailor guide detailing vibrant trade in the Satavahana realm.
  • Western Ports: Barygaza (Broach), Kalyan, Sopara, and Chaul connecting with Alexandria and Rome.
  • Eastern Coromandel Ports:
    ▫ Maisolos (Machilipatnam): Renowned for exporting world-class gossamer-thin muslin cloth.
    ▫ Kantakosyla (Ghantasala), Koduru, and Mottupalli: Active trade hubs for Southeast Asian trans-shipment.
  • Roman Trade Imbalance: Exported muslins, spices, silks, pearls, and diamonds; imported wine amphorae, glass, copper, tin, and vast volumes of Roman gold aurei and silver denarii (Pliny lamented this drain of Roman wealth).
  • Coinage: Minted coins in Lead, Potin (copper-zinc alloy), Copper, and Silver (Karshapanas). Silver was largely reserved for commercial trade in the northern domains.
V. Socio-Cultural Life, Religion, Literature & Art

1. Social Life & Status of Women

  • Four Classes: Society stratified by profession into Maharathis/Mahabhojas (top tier), royal officials and merchants (2nd tier), agriculturalists/artisans (3rd tier), and manual laborers (4th tier).
  • Matronymics: Kings took names derived from their mothers (e.g., Gautami-putra, Vasishthi-putra), reflecting elevated domestic respect and matriarchal reverence, though the succession itself remained strictly patrilineal.
  • Status of Women: Royal ladies enjoyed high property ownership, issued personal religious endowments, managed charities, and served as regents during minority successions (e.g., Queen Naganika).

2. Religion: Buddhism & Vedic Revival

  • Brahmanical Revival: Kings actively patronized orthodox Vedic rites (Asvamedha, Gavamayana, Aptoryama) and worshipped Vasudeva-Krishna, Siva, Indra, and Surya.
  • Flourishing of Buddhism:
    ▫ State maintained religious tolerance; merchant classes and royal women heavily subsidized Mahayana Buddhist institutions.
    ▫ Major sects: Chaityakas, Purvasailas, and Aparasailas centered along the Krishna valley.
    ▫ Acharya Nagarjuna: Expounded Madhyamika (Sunyavada) philosophy at Sriparvata (Nagarjunakonda); patronized by King Yajna Sri Satakarni (for whom he wrote Suhrullekha).

3. Language & Literature

  • Official Language: Prakrit was the court and inscriptional language; written in the Brahmi script.
  • Gathasaptasati (Sattasai): 700 verses in Maharashtri Prakrit compiled by King Hala, capturing everyday village life, agriculture, love, and folk culture.
  • Brihatkatha: Masterpiece authored by Gunadhya in the Paisachi Prakrit dialect; laid the base for the later Sanskrit Kathasaritsagara.
  • Katantra Vyakarana: Sanskrit grammar treatise composed by Sarvavarman to help King Hala master Sanskrit rapidly.
  • Early Telugu Elements: Prakrit inscriptions and Hala's poetry contain early Telugu words (Desi vocabulary like Atta, Pilla, Vanta, and the inscriptional term Nagabu at Amaravati).

4. Art & Architecture: Amaravati School

  • Amaravati School of Art: Indigenous art school flourishing along the lower Krishna; primarily utilized fine-grained white marble / Palnadu limestone.
  • Stylistic Features: Noted for natural grace, physical movement, emotional expressiveness, narrative friezes, and circular medallion carvings depicting Jataka stories.
  • Maha Chaitya at Amaravati:
    ▫ Solid dome (Anda) standing on a high circular drum.
    ▫ Distinctive Ayaka Platforms projecting at the four cardinal directions, each crowned by five Ayaka Pillars (symbolizing 5 major events of Buddha's life: Birth, Renunciation, Enlightenment, First Sermon, Parinirvana).
  • Cave Architecture: Excavation of rock-cut Chaityas (prayer halls) and Viharas (monasteries) at Karle, Bhaja, Kanheri, and Bedsa in the Western Ghats; Karle Chaitya is the largest and most magnificent rock-cut prayer hall in India.
  • TRAP 1: "The Satavahanas used Telugu as their official inscriptional language." ➔ FALSE! Their official language was Prakrit, and the script was Brahmi. Telugu appeared only as loan words and personal names.
  • TRAP 2: "Matronymics indicate that the Satavahanas were a matriarchal society." ➔ FALSE! Throne succession was strictly patrilineal (father to son). Matronymics (naming after mothers) indicated social and domestic respect for women, not female rule.
  • TRAP 3: "The Satavahanas minted gold coins for international trade." ➔ FALSE! The Satavahanas never issued regular gold coins. They minted Lead, Potin, Copper, and Silver coins. International trade was settled with imported Roman gold coins (Aurei).
  • TRAP 4: "Gautamiputra Satakarni founded the Satavahana dynasty." ➔ FALSE! The dynasty was founded by Simuka. Gautamiputra was the 23rd ruler who restored the dynasty after foreign Saka occupations.
  • TRAP 5: "The earliest epigraphic evidence of tax-free land gifts is from the Gupta period." ➔ FALSE! It is found in the Naneghat Inscription of Queen Naganika (Satavahana era), recording land grants to priests and monks.
Chronological Sequence Hook:
Simuka (Founder) ➔ Satakarni I (Vedic Sacrifices/Naneghat) ➔ Hala (Gathasaptasati/Prakrit) ➔ Gautamiputra Satakarni (Crushed Sakas/Ekabrahmana) ➔ Pulumavi (Amaravati Capital) ➔ Yajna Sri Satakarni (Ship-type Coins/Naval Trade) ➔ Disintegration into Ikshvakus, Pallavas & Abhiras.
