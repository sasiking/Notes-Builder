II. European Trade Settlements & British Territorial Annexation

1. European Factories along the Andhra Coast

  • Dutch East India Company (VOC): Established their first Indian factory at Machilipatnam (1605) followed by Nizampatnam (Petapuli) and Pulicat; dominated the export of fine indigo, rice, and diamond transit.
  • English East India Company (EIC):
    ▫ Opened their first operational southeastern factory at Machilipatnam (1611) aboard the ship Globe.
    ▫ Secured the landmark Golden Farman (1632) from Sultan Abdullah Qutb Shah, allowing duty-free commerce for 500 pagodas annually.
    ▫ Established trade settlements at Armagon (Dugarajapatnam, 1626) and Visakhapatnam (1682).
  • French & Other Powers: French built trade centers at Machilipatnam and Yanam (1723); Portuguese operated out of coastal San Thome and Masulipatnam pockets.

2. Acquisition of Northern Circars & Ceded Districts

  • The Northern Circars (5 Coastal Districts):
    ▫ Comprised Chicacole (Srikakulam), Rajahmundry, Eluru, Mustafanagar (Kondapalli), and Murtazanagar (Guntur).
    ▫ 1753: Nizam Salabat Jung ceded Circars to French General Bussy.
    ▫ 1759: Colonel Forde defeated the French at the Battle of Chandurthi (Condore); captured Machilipatnam.
    ▫ 1765/1766: Robert Clive secured an Imperial Farman from Mughal Emperor Shah Alam II; ratified by Nizam Ali Khan under the Treaty of Hyderabad (Guntur ceded later in 1788).
  • The Ceded Districts (Rayalaseema):
    ▫ Bellary, Anantapur, Cuddapah, and Kurnool surrendered to the British by Nizam Ali Khan in 1800 under the Subsidiary Alliance to fund British subsidiary troops.
III. Land Systems & Feudal Resistance: Zamindars & Poligars

1. Zamindari System & Revolts in Circars

  • Permanent Settlement (1802): Imposed in coastal Circars by Lord Wellesley; converted traditional revenue collectors and local chieftains into hereditary proprietary landlords with fixed revenue demands.
  • Battle of Padmanabham (1794):
    ▫ Conflict erupted when the EIC ordered Raja Chinna Vijayarama Raju of Vizianagaram to disband his troops and clear excessive tribute arrears.
    ▫ The Raja fought British forces under Colonel Prendergast and died on the battlefield at Padmanabham; state was subsequently partitioned and subordinated.
  • Draksharama & Hill Zamindari Revolts: Consistent uprisings led by hill chieftains (e.g., Payakrao of Palakonda, Virabhadra Raju) against British revenue extractions.

2. The Poligar System & Rayalaseema Rebellions

  • Poligars (Palegars): Feudal military chieftains who held armed forts and gathered village revenues in exchange for maintaining armed troops (legacy of the Vijayanagara Nayankara system).
  • Munro's Pacification Campaign (1800–1801): Major Thomas Munro forcefully disarmed 80 regional poligars, dismantled their stone forts, and confiscated independent private armies to enforce public order.
  • Uyyalawada Narasimha Reddy's Rebellion (1846):
    ▫ Renowned Poligar of Koilkuntla (Kurnool); revolted against the arbitrary reduction of traditional Kattubadi pensions and agrarian distress.
    ▫ Led an armed guerrilla rebellion of thousands across Koilkuntla, Giddalur, and Nallamala forests; captured and publicly hanged in 1847.
IV. Colonial Scholars, Administrators & Missionary Impact

1. Munro, Mackenzie & C.P. Brown

  • Sir Thomas Munro: Principal Collector of the Ceded Districts (1800–1807) and Governor of Madras; instituted the Ryotwari System (direct state-peasant settlement, eliminating intermediaries); revered by peasants as "Munrolappa".
  • Colin Mackenzie: First Surveyor General of India; amassed the famous Mackenzie Manuscripts (Kaifiyats, village land charters, local histories, stone inscriptions) preserving valuable Andhra regional history.
  • Charles Philip Brown (C.P. Brown):
    ▫ British civilian judge known as the savior of classical Telugu literature.
    ▫ Rescued, edited, and printed decaying palm-leaf manuscripts including Vemana Satakamu (1829), Andhra Mahabharatamu, and Palnati Vira Charitra.
    ▫ Compiled the comprehensive English-Telugu Dictionary (1852) and established free libraries in Cuddapah.

2. Role of Christian Missionaries & Western Education

  • Key Missionary Societies: London Missionary Society (Visakhapatnam, 1805), Church Missionary Society (Masulipatnam, 1841), American Evangelical Lutheran Mission (Guntur, 1842 under John Christian Frederick Heyer).
  • Noble College (Machilipatnam, 1843): Established by Rev. Robert Noble; pioneered modern higher English education in Andhra.
  • Social and Linguistic Awakenings:
    ▫ Established modern printing presses, casting Telugu typesets for translation of biblical and secular texts.
    ▫ Opened early modern schools for depressed classes (Dalits/Panchamas) and women, challenging age-old social exclusion.
    ▫ Prompted orthodox Hindu society to establish reform associations to counter proselytization.

VI. Library Movement, Nationalist Press & Role of Women

1. The Library Movement (Granthalaya Udyamam)

  • Inception: First public modern library established as Sri Krishna Devaraya Andhra Bhasha Nilayam (1901) at Ramkote, Hyderabad, by Komarraju Lakshmana Rao and Nayani Venkata Ranga Rao.
  • Grassroots Spread in Coastal Andhra:
    ▫ Sri Rajaraja Narendra Andhra Bhasha Nilayam (1904): Founded at Hanamkonda.
    ▫ Andhra Desa Granthalaya Sangham (1914): Established at Vijayawada; first state-level library association in India, pioneered by Ayyanki Venkata Ramanayya and Suraneni Venkata Narasimha Rao.
    ▫ Boat Libraries & Walking Libraries: Libraries circulated books on canal boats and bullock carts across Godavari delta villages to spread mass literacy and national consciousness.

2. Role of the Press & Political Journalism

  • Early Pioneers: Vrittanta Manjari (1838, Madras), Hitavadi, and Veeresalingam's reformist journal Vivekavardhani (1874).
  • Andhra Patrika:
    ▫ Founded as a weekly in Bombay (1908) by Kasunathuni Nageswara Rao Pantulu; shifted to Madras as a daily in 1914.
    ▫ Acted as the primary medium of political nationalist awareness and cultural revivalism across Telugu homes.
  • Krishna Patrika (1902): Founded at Machilipatnam by Konda Venkatappayya; edited by Mutnuri Krishna Rao; served as an intellectual center for the Swadeshi and Vande Mataram movements.

3. Awakening and Role of Women

  • Pioneering Women Leaders:
    ▫ Kallakuri Rajyalakshmamma: Actively worked alongside her husband Veeresalingam, managing the Widow Home (Vidhava Saranalayam) in Rajahmundry.
    ▫ Duvvuri Subbamma: Fearless freedom fighter from East Godavari; first Andhra woman to undergo rigorous prison sentences for nationalist speeches; led civil disobedience.
    ▫ Ponaka Kanakamma: Founded the Sri Kasturidevi Vidyalayam for girls at Nellore (1923), inspired by Mahatma Gandhi.
    ▫ Dr. Durgabai Deshmukh: Organized the Andhra Mahila Sabha in Madras (1937), training thousands of destitute women in nursing, crafts, and social service.
VII. Folk Traditions, Tribal Movements & Subaltern Culture

1. Folk Arts & Oral Performance Traditions

  • Burra Katha: Dynamic narrative musical storytelling using the Tambura and Andelu; widely adopted during national and communist movements to mobilize rural peasants.
  • Oggu Katha: Traditional oral ballad performance among the Kuruma and Golla shepherd communities, chanting heroic pastoral epics of Mallanna and Beerappa.
  • Tolu Bommalata (Shadow Puppetry): Rural leather puppet theaters dramatizing episodes from the Ramayana and Mahabharata, surviving predominantly around Nimmalakunta (Anantapur).
  • Jamukula Katha & Harikatha: Itinerant balladeers and storytellers bringing philosophical, historical, and mythological lessons directly to village platforms.

2. Tribal Identity & The Manyam Rebellion

  • Tribal Grievances: Imposition of the 1882 Madras Forest Act banned traditional Podu (slash-and-burn) cultivation, restricted forest produce collection, and introduced the oppressive Muttadari extraction system.
  • Alluri Sitarama Raju (1922–1924):
    ▫ Led the celebrated Rampa Rebellion (Manyam Udyamam) in the Eastern Ghats among Koya and Konda Dora tribesmen.
    ▫ Executed strategic guerrilla attacks on British police stations (Chintapalli, Krishnadevipeta, Rampachodavaram) to seize arms.
    ▫ Empathized with subaltern forest dwellers; combined ascetic spiritual devotion with modern armed resistance; captured and martyred in May 1924.
  • TRAP 1: "Thomas Munro introduced the Zamindari system in the Ceded Districts." ➔ FALSE! Munro was a fierce opponent of the Zamindari intermediary model; he introduced the Ryotwari system (direct agreement with the cultivator). Permanent Settlement Zamindari was introduced in the Northern Circars in 1802.
  • TRAP 2: "The first public Telugu library was established in Vijayawada." ➔ FALSE! The earliest modern public library was Sri Krishna Devaraya Andhra Bhasha Nilayam (1901) established at Hyderabad (Ramkote). Vijayawada hosted the formation of the Andhra Desa Granthalaya Sangham in 1914.
  • TRAP 3: "Gurajada Apparao's Kanyasulkam was written in classical literary Grandhika Telugu." ➔ FALSE! Gurajada wrote Kanyasulkam deliberately in Vyavaharika Bhasha (spoken dialect) to expose social evils and prove that spoken language is capable of elite dramatic expression.
  • TRAP 4: "C.P. Brown translated Vemana's verses in the 20th century." ➔ FALSE! C.P. Brown translated and published Vemana's poems in 1829 (early 19th century).
  • TRAP 5: "The first English factory in India was established at Machilipatnam." ➔ FALSE! It was the Company's first factory on the southeastern coast / Bay of Bengal (1611). Surat (1608/1612) was their early hub on the western coast.
