Religious Reform Movements: Jainism & Buddhism (c. 6th Century BCE)

Socio-Economic Genesis • Philosophy • Councils & Literature • Comparative Synthesis
I. The 6th Century BCE Intellectual Ferment & Material Milieu

Raise of New Religions

  • Purpose: 
    • 64 new heterodox religion are formed to oppose Cattle slaughter, Varna system, Brahminism monopolym Sanskrit sacrifices
  • Important Religions: Jainism, Buddhism, Ajeevika, Charvakas.

1. Material Roots (The Second Urbanization)

  • Iron Implements (Krishna Ayas): Clearance of dense forests in the middle Gangetic plains resulted in settled agriculture with extensive crop surpluses.
  • Preservation of Cattle Wealth: Intensive agrarian ploughing necessitated draught animals. Brahmanical animal sacrifices (Pashubali) depleted bovine assets essential for food security.
  • Rise of the Gahapatis & Setthis: Wealthy urban merchants and landowners sought a philosophical system free from ritual degradation of usury/money lending (Kusida), prohibited by Vedic Dharmasutras.
  • Use of Money: Punch-marked coins (silver/copper) enhanced trade fluidity; religious asceticism accommodated the changing commercial reality.

2. Kshatriya Reaction & Sramana Traditions

  • Kshatriya-Brahmana Rivalry: Both Vardhamana Mahavira and Gautama Buddha belonged to royal Kshatriya lineages (Jnatrika and Shakya clans), challenging the ritual supremacy of the priestly class.
  • Sramana Movement: Wandering philosophers and seekers rejecting Vedic authority, rituals, and the caste monopoly.
    ▫ Ajivikas: Founded by Makkhali Gosala; strict determinism/fatalism (Niyati); denied human free will.
    ▫ Charvakas / Lokayatas: Founded by Charvaka / Ajita Kesakambalin; radical materialism; rejected soul, god, and afterlife.
    ▫ Akrityavada: Purana Kassapa; actions yield no moral merit or demerit.
    ▫ Ucchedavada: Pakudha Kaccayana; eternal existence of 7 uncreated elements.
📌 Vernacular Appeal: Both traditions decisively abandoned esoteric Sanskrit in favor of spoken dialects — Ardhamagadhi / Prakrit for Jainism and Pali for Buddhism.

2. Core Buddhist Philosophy

  • Arya Satya (Four Noble Truths):
    1. Dukkha: The world is full of suffering.
    2. Dukkha Samudaya: Cause of suffering is desire (Tanha / Trishna).
    3. Dukkha Nirodha: Cessation of suffering is possible.
    4. Dukkha Nirodha Gamini Pratipada: Path leading to the cessation of suffering (Ashtangika Marga).
  • Madhyama Pratipada: Rejection of severe asceticism (Jainism) and extreme sensual indulgence (Vedic ritualism); adherence to the "Middle Path".
  • Pratityasamutpada (Dependent Origination): "This arising, that arises"; 12-linked chain of causation (Dvadasa Nidana).
  • Metaphysical Stances:
    ▫ Anatmavada: Denial of a permanent, eternal soul (Atman).
    ▫ Anityavada / Kshanikavada: Theory of transience; everything changes every moment.
    ▫ Silent on the existence of God (Atheistic/Agnostic).

IV. Jainism: Tirthankaras, Philosophy & Canonical Traditions

1. Tirthankaras & Mahavira's Life

  • 24 Tirthankaras ("Ford-makers"):
    ▫ 1st (Rishabhanatha / Adinatha): Bull symbol; mentioned in Rigveda and Vayu Purana.
    ▫ 22nd (Arishtanemi): Conch symbol; mentioned alongside Lord Krishna in Rigveda.
    ▫ 23rd (Parshvanatha): Serpent symbol; prince of Benaras who gave the 4 vows (Ahimsa, Satya, Asteya, Aparigraha).
    ▫ 24th (Vardhamana Mahavira): Lion symbol; added the 5th vow (Brahmacharya).
  • Chronological Milestones of Mahavira (540 – 468 BCE):
    ▫ Birth: Kundagrama near Vaishali; Jnatrika Kshatriya clan; father Siddhartha, mother Trishala (sister of Lichchhavi chief Chetaka).
    ▫ Renunciation: Age 30 (after parents' demise with brother Nandivardhana's consent).
    ▫ Kaivalya (Supreme Knowledge): At age 42, on the banks of river Rijupalika under a Sal tree near Jrimbhikagrama.
    ▫ Mahaparinirvana: At age 72 at Pavapuri near Rajgriha (court of King Hastipala).

2. Epistemology, Metaphysics & Conduct

  • Tri-Ratna (Three Jewels):
    1. Samyak Darshana: Right Faith / Perception.
    2. Samyak Jnana: Right Knowledge.
    3. Samyak Charitra: Right Conduct (adherence to 5 Mahavratas).
  • The Five Mahavratas: Ahimsa (non-violence), Satya (truth), Asteya (non-stealing), Aparigraha (non-possession), Brahmacharya (celibacy).
  • Relativistic Epistemology:
    ▫ Anekantavada: Doctrine of the manifold nature of reality (pluralism).
    ▫ Syadvada: The theory of conditioned predication ("may be" / 7-fold judgment or Saptabhangi Naya).
  • Hylozoism: Belief that all matter, even stones, water, fire, and wind, possesses souls (Jiva).
  • Sallekhana / Santhara: Religious vow of voluntary fasting to death (Chandragupta Maurya undertook it at Shravanabelagola).

V. Comprehensive Comparison: Jainism vs. Buddhism
Comparative Axis Jainism Buddhism
Concept of Soul (Atman) Radical Soul-Realism: Believes in eternal souls (Jiva) existing in all animate and inanimate objects (hylozoism). Anatmavada (No-Soul): Denies the existence of a permanent soul; self is a bundle of 5 shifting aggregates (Skandhas).
Austerity & Ahimsa Extreme asceticism: Complete renunciation of clothes (Digambara), extreme non-violence (even covering mouth, avoiding agriculture/root vegetables). Middle Path (Madhyama Marga): Rejects self-mortification; permits moderate living, seasonal clothes, and normal agriculture.
Liberation / Salvation Kaivalya: Freedom of soul from the contamination of physical Karma, often through voluntary mortification/fasting (Sallekhana). Nirvana: Extinction of desire and ignorance during life; blowing out the flame of craving without physical torture.
Varna / Caste Outlook Does NOT unequivocally condemn the Varna system; Mahavira stated one's birth in a high or low caste was due to sins/merits in past life. Explicitly attacks the ritual basis of the Varna system; opened Sangha gates to all castes without distinction.
Admission of Women Svetambaras admit women; believe 19th Tirthankara (Malli) was female. Digambaras deny women can attain Kaivalya without being reborn as men. Admitted women to the Bhikkhuni Sangha at the insistence of Ananda and Mahapajapati Gotami (though Buddha gave strict Gurudharmas).
Geographical Spread Remained confined strictly within the boundaries of the Indian subcontinent (Rajasthan, Gujarat, Karnataka). Spread dynamically across Asia as a global religion (Sri Lanka, Tibet, China, Japan, Southeast Asia).
Canonical Language Ardhamagadhi Prakrit (later Sanskrit by Hemachandra). Pali for early/Theravada; Classical Sanskrit adopted in Mahayana.

VII. Exam-Trick Boxes (Avoid Common Traps!)
  • TRAP 1: "Vardhamana Mahavira was the founder of Jainism." ➔ WRONG! Rishabhanatha was the 1st Tirthankara and founder. Mahavira was the 24th and last Tirthankara who codified and institutionalized it.
  • TRAP 2: "Both Buddhism and Jainism rejected the doctrine of Karma and Rebirth." ➔ WRONG! Both religions strictly accepted the law of Karma and the cycle of Rebirth (Punarjanma). They only rejected the infallible authority of the Vedas, animal sacrifice, and priestly intermediaries.
  • TRAP 3: "Buddhism believes in the transmigration of the permanent soul (Atman)." ➔ WRONG! Buddhism vehemently upholds Anatmavada (no permanent soul). Rebirth in Buddhism is the continuity of karmic consciousness (stream of consciousness), like one candle lighting another, NOT a migrating eternal Atman.
  • TRAP 4: "The doctrine of Anekantavada belongs to Buddhism." ➔ WRONG! Anekantavada (plurality of truth) and Syadvada (relative truth) are the hallmark philosophies of Jainism. Do not confuse it with Buddhist Kshanikavada (momentariness) or Sunyavada (emptiness).
  • TRAP 5: "Women were admitted into the Buddhist Sangha from the very first sermon." ➔ WRONG! The Buddha was initially reluctant. The female monastic order (Bhikkhuni Sangha) was founded years later at Vaishali only after intense pleading by his foster mother Mahapajapati Gotami and disciple Ananda.

Mains Practice • 10 Marks Q1. "Buddhism and Jainism were not merely religious protests, but social and economic movements that reflected the material transitions of their age." Analyze.
View Model Answer Structuring Framework
Model Structuring Framework:
• Introduction: Characterize the 6th century BCE as the dawn of the "Second Urbanization" in the middle Gangetic basin, marked by surplus crop yields, craft guilds, and trade expansion that came into sharp friction with the pastoral, sacrificial ethos of Later Vedic Brahmanism.
• Economic Dimension:
▫ Iron Plough Agriculture: Required draft cattle. Brahmanical yajnas slaughtered cattle indiscriminately; both religions made non-violence (Ahimsa) their cardinal principle, protecting bovine assets.
▫ Legitimizing Trade & Usury: Orthodox Dharmasutras condemned seafaring and money-lending (Kusida); Buddhism and Jainism accommodated business interests, leading to patronage by Setthis and Gahapatis.
• Social Dimension:
▫ Challenge to Varna Hierarchy: Kshatriyas resented Brahmana ritual hegemony; merchants (Vaishyas) desired social status commensurate with their wealth.
▫ Democratic Inclusivity: Buddhism and Jainism admitted lower varnas and women into their monastic orders, replacing birth-based hierarchy with ethical moral conduct (Sila).
▫ Vernacular Medium: Replaced formal Sanskrit with the language of the masses (Pali and Ardhamagadhi), democratizing access to sacred philosophy.
• Conclusion: These movements succeeded because they offered an ideological framework that harmonized with the socio-economic imperatives of an urbanizing, commercial society.
Mains Practice • 15 Marks Q2. "Assess the philosophical differences between Jainism and Buddhism with special reference to their attitudes toward asceticism, the concept of the soul, and salvation."
View Model Answer Structuring Framework
Model Structuring Framework:
• Introduction: Both Jainism and Buddhism arose as Sramana traditions challenging Vedic infallibility, yet evolved distinct metaphysical paradigms concerning spiritual liberation.
• Philosophical Dialectic:
▫ Asceticism vs. Middle Way: Jainism advocates severe bodily mortification, nakedness (Digambara), and ritual starvation (Sallekhana) to purge past karma. Buddhism unequivocally rejects severe mortification as futile, adopting Madhyama Pratipada (Middle Path).
▫ Jiva (Soul) vs. Anatmavada (No-Soul): Jainism is deeply hylozoic, asserting that all physical matter contains an eternal, individual soul (Jiva). Buddhism propounds Anatmavada, denying an eternal, unchanging soul, viewing the individual as a constantly shifting configuration of 5 aggregates (Skandhas).
▫ Kaivalya vs. Nirvana: Kaivalya represents the purification and upward ascent of the soul to the apex of the universe (Siddhashila). Nirvana in Buddhism is psychological emancipation—the extinction of the flame of craving (Tanha) and attainment of peace during life.
▫ Epistemology: Jainism developed relativism via Anekantavada and Syadvada; Buddhism developed causal dynamism via Pratityasamutpada and Kshanikavada (momentariness).
• Conclusion: While Jainism's severe austerity prevented wide foreign adoption, its extreme ethical strictness preserved it within India; conversely, Buddhism's pragmatic flexibility enabled it to transcend geographic boundaries and emerge as a world religion.
Mains Practice • 10 Marks Q3. "Explain the role of Guilds in 6th century BCE"
View Model Answer Structuring Framework
Answer Framework:
• Introduction: The 6th century BCE marked the dawn of the Second Urbanisation in the middle Gangetic basin, characterized by agricultural surpluses, monetization via punch-marked coins, and the expansion of craft specialization.
▫ Note: As specialized artisanal manufacture outgrew domestic production, craftsmen and merchants organized into corporate associations known as Srenis (guilds), Nigamas, and Pugas, headed by influential leaders called Pramukhas, Jyestakas, or Shresthi.

• Every Guild turned into a caste and consolidated the caste system.
• Core Analysis:
▫ Economic Organization & Quality Control: Guilds bought raw materials in bulk, trained young apprentices, set fixed product prices, and checked the quality of goods like cloth, metal tools, pots, and woodwork. 
▫ Early Banking & Loans: Srenis took cash deposits from kings and the public on interest, gave loans to traders, and funded long-distance bullock caravan trade on the Uttarapatha and Dakshinapatha routes. 
▫ Internal Rules & Justice: Guilds functioned under their own customary laws (Srenidharma); local kings recognized these customs, allowing guild heads to resolve conflicts between their own members without royal interference. 
▫ Support to Buddhism & Jainism: Traders (Setthis and Vaishyas) disliked the strict Vedic caste hierarchy that looked down on interest-earning money-lending (Kusida); to gain social respect, guilds donated heavily to build monasteries (viharas), rest halls, and stupas for Buddhist and Jain monks. 
▫ City Building & Welfare: Guilds helped build urban centers by digging public water wells and tanks, running free food kitchens, constructing rest houses, and maintaining town defenses in capitals like Rajagriha, Kaushambi, and Shravasti.

• Conclusion: By moving economic life away from simple barter into organized business unions, 6th-century BCE guilds created the wealth and stability needed for early kingdoms to grow into the Mauryan Empire.
