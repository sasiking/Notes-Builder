Andhra Pradesh History: Ancient to Early Medieval Outline

Satavahanas to Vishnukundins • Eastern Chalukyas, Rashtrakutas & Renati Cholas • Socio-Economic, Religious & Linguistic Evolution
I. Ancient Dynasties of Andhra (Political Outline)
Dynasty Capital / Core Region Prominent Rulers Key Historical Significance
Satavahanas
(225 Bc - 225 AD)		 Kotilingala, Dhanyakataka (Amaravati), Pratishthana Simukha, Satakarni I, Hala, Gautamiputra Satakarni, Yajna Sri Satakarni Unified the Deccan; introduced royal land grants (Brahmadeya); flourishing Indo-Roman trade; patrons of Prakrit literature and Amaravati art.
Ikshvakus
(225 AD - 200 AD)		 Vijayapuri (Nagarjunakonda) Vasishthiputra Santamula, Virapurushadatta, Ehuvula Santamula Successors of Satavahanas in Krishna valley; kings patronized Vedic sacrifices while royal ladies extensively patronized Mahayana Buddhism.
Salankayanas
(200 AD - 420 AD)		 Vengipura (Pedavegi, near Eluru) Hastivarman (contemporary of Samudragupta), Nandivarman Replaced the Ikshvakus in Vengi region; recognized by the totem of the bull (Chitrarathaswami); key transition from Prakrit to Sanskrit charters.
Early Pallavas
(c. 4th Cent – 6th Cent CE)		 Kanchipuram (held Southern Andhra: Guntur, Nellore) Simhavarman, Sivaskandavarman, Vishnugopa Issued early Prakrit and Sanskrit copper plates in Andhra (Mayidavolu, Omgodu charters); established bureaucratic administrative patterns.
Vishnukundins
(c. 5th Cent – 7th Cent CE)		 Amrabad, Vinukonda, Denduluru Madhavavarma II (Janashraya), Govindavarman Champions of Brahmanical revival (performed Asvamedha, Purushamedha); carved rock-cut cave temples at Mogalrajapuram and Undavalli.
II. Ancient Social & Economic Conditions

1. Social Structure & Status of Women

  • Varna System: Four-fold division existed, but traditional caste rigidity was flexible due to dominant merchant classes and Buddhist ethos.
  • Matronymics: Satavahana kings used mothers' names (e.g., Gautamiputra, Vashishtiputra), demonstrating high social reverence for maternal lineage.
  • Role of Women: Royal women enjoyed property rights, issued independent donations, built viharas, and controlled religious philanthropy (especially among Ikshvaku queens like Chamtisri).
  • Joint Family System: Patriarchal nuclear and joint households (Grihas) formed the base, with the Grihapati as social unit head.

2. Agrarian & Commercial Economy

  • Merchant Guilds (Srenis/Nikayas): Autonomous craft and merchant guilds operated as banks, receiving deposits, paying interest, and regulating production.
  • Internal & Maritime Trade: Extensive maritime contacts with the Roman Empire through ports like Motupalli, Ghantasala, and Koduru.
  • Currency Economy: Circulation of Potin, Lead, Copper, and Silver coins (Karshapanas); abundant Roman gold aurei found along coastal Andhra.
  • Land Grants: Introduction of tax-free land donations to priests and Buddhist monasteries, laying early foundations for localized agrarian estates.
III. Religion, Language, Literature, Art & Architecture

1. Religion: Buddhism, Jainism & Brahmanism

  • Buddhism in Andhra:
    ▫ Flourished under state and guild patronage; major sects included Chaityakas, Purvasailas, and Aparasailas.
    ▫ Acharya Nagarjuna: Expounded the Madhyamika (Sunyavada) philosophy at Sriparvata (Nagarjunakonda).
  • Jainism in Andhra:
    ▫ Early centers at Munugodu, Vaddamanu; Acharya Kundakunda preached Digambara Jainism from Konakondla.
  • Vedic & Puranic Revival:
    ▫ Kings performed Vedic sacrifices (Asvamedha, Rajasuya, Gavamayana).
    ▫ Gradual rise of Shaivism and Vaishnavism under the Vishnukundins (Sriparvataswami as family deity).

2. Language, Literature & Epigraphy

  • Court Language: Prakrit was the official language of early royal inscriptions; Sanskrit gained primacy under the late Ikshvakus and Vishnukundins.
  • Early Telugu Traces:
    ▫ Archaic Telugu words found embedded in Hala's Prakrit anthology Gathasaptasati (e.g., Atta, Vanta, Pilla).
    ▫ Amaravati inscription (1st Cent CE) contains the word "Nagabu", representing early Telugu linguistic traces.
  • Key Literary Works:
    ▫ Gathasaptasati by King Hala (Maharashtri Prakrit).
    ▫ Brihatkatha by Gunadhya (Paisachi Prakrit).
    ▫ Katantra Vyakarana by Sarvavarman (Sanskrit grammar).
    ▫ Suhrullekha and Prajnaparamita by Nagarjuna.

3. Art and Architecture: Amaravati School & Cave Temples

  • Amaravati School of Art: Indigenous aesthetic school using white marble/limestone; noted for physical grace, human emotion, dynamic narrative relief panels, and medallion carvings.
  • Stupa Architecture: Maha Chaitya at Amaravati, Bhattiprolu, Ghantasala, and Salihundam; distinctive features include Ayaka platforms with five Ayaka pillars representing five events in Buddha's life.
  • Nagarjunakonda Architecture: Unique coexistence of Buddhist stupas, viharas, and chaityas alongside earliest Brahmanical brick shrines (Pushpabhadraswami temple, Sarvadeva temple, Asvamedha arena).
  • Rock-Cut Cave Architecture: Vishnukundins introduced rock-cut cave temples in Andhra at Undavalli (Anantasayana Vishnu) and Mogalrajapuram (Trikuta cave and Nataraja relief).
IV. Early Medieval Dynasties: Eastern Chalukyas, Rashtrakutas & Renati Cholas

1. Eastern Chalukyas of Vengi (c. 624 – 1070 CE)

  • Founding: Established by Kubja Vishnuvardhana (brother of Badami Chalukya ruler Pulakesin II).
  • Prominent Monarchs: Jayasimha I, Vijayaditya II (Narendra Mrigaraja, credited with building 108 Shiva temples), Gunaga Vijayaditya, Rajaraja Narendra.
  • Administrative Shift: Shifted capital from Vengipura to Rajahmundry; transformed Andhra into a unified geopolitical linguistic entity (Vengi Mandala).
  • Matrimonial Ties: Extensive marital alliances with the Imperial Cholas, culminating in Kulottunga Chola I uniting the Vengi and Chola crowns.

2. Renati Cholas & Rashtrakuta Hegemony

  • Renati Cholas (Rayalaseema Region):
    ▫ Ruled Renadu (Cuddapah, Kurnool, Chittoor regions).
    ▫ Famous ruler: Erikal Mutthuraju Dhananjaya.
    ▫ Issued the celebrated Kalamalla Inscription (c. 575 CE), the earliest complete epigraph in Telugu prose.
  • Rashtrakutas in Andhra:
    ▫ Continual power struggles with Eastern Chalukyas for control of the fertile Vengi and Krishna-Tungabhadra Doabs.
    ▫ Rulers like Govinda III and Amoghavarsha I exercised overlordship over western Telangana and Rayalaseema pockets.
V. Socio-Cultural Life, Religion & Architecture of the Medieval Era

1. Medieval Society & Religion

  • Decline of Heterodox Faiths: Buddhism practically disappeared; Jainism retained patronage in isolated trade hubs and hill shrines (e.g., Danavulapadu in Kadapa district).
  • Triumph of Puranic Hinduism:
    ▫ Widespread adoption of Shaivism (Pasupata, Kalamukha sects) and Vaishnavism.
    ▫ Temple became the nucleus of economic, religious, and cultural activity (managing lands, dancing schools, hospitals).
  • Social Stratification: Proliferation of sub-castes (Jatis), rise of feudal landed intermediaries (Samantas, Mahasamantas), and development of village service groups.

2. Rise of Classical Telugu Literature & Epigraphy

  • Birth of Classical Literature:
    ▫ Nannaya Bhattu (11th Cent CE): Court poet of Rajaraja Narendra; revered as Adi Kavi; translated initial cantos of the Andhra Mahabharatamu (Adi, Sabha, part of Aranya Parva).
    ▫ Author of Andhra Sabda Chintamani, establishing early Telugu grammatical standards.
  • Milestone Epigraphs:
    ▫ Kalamalla Inscription (c. 575 CE): First standalone Telugu inscription by Renati Cholas.
    ▫ Addanki Inscription of Panduranga (848 CE): Earliest occurrence of Telugu metrical poetry (Taruvoja verse).
    ▫ Bezwada Inscription of Yuddhamalla: Earliest instance of the Madhyakkara meter in Telugu verse.

3. Art & Architecture: Chalukyan Temple Traditions

  • Draksharama & Kumararama (Bhimavaram): Monumental Pancharama temples constructed during the reign of Eastern Chalukya King Bhima I; feature colossal, two-story tall granite Shiva lingas.
  • Biccavolu Temple Group: Exceptional early Chalukyan stone shrines (Golingeswara, Raja Raja, Chandrasekhara temples) reflecting a blend of Nagara spires with southern Dravidian decorative niches.
  • Alampur Nava Brahma Temples: Situated along the Tungabhadra; pristine execution of Rekha-Nagara vimanas showcasing Badami and early medieval Chalukya architectural idioms.
  • TRAP 1: "Telugu became the court language under the Satavahanas." ➔ FALSE! Satavahanas used Prakrit for administration and inscriptions. Early Telugu existed primarily as spoken dialects and place/proper names.
  • TRAP 2: "The earliest Telugu inscription was issued by the Eastern Chalukyas." ➔ FALSE! The earliest standalone Telugu inscription is the Kalamalla Inscription (c. 575 CE) issued by the Renati Cholas.
  • TRAP 3: "Ikshvaku kings were fervent Mahayana Buddhists." ➔ FALSE! The Ikshvaku kings performed orthodox Vedic sacrifices (Asvamedha, Vajapeya); it was the royal female consorts and princesses who patronized Buddhism at Nagarjunakonda.
  • TRAP 4: "Nannaya translated the entire Mahabharata into Telugu." ➔ FALSE! Nannaya translated only 2.5 cantos (Adi, Sabha, and part of Aranya Parva). The epic was later completed by Tikkana (13th Cent) and Errana (14th Cent), forming the Kavitrayam.
