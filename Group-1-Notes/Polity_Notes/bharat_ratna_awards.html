I. Core Rules, Age Extremes & Award Limits

1. Basic Award Architecture

  • Started: Instituted on 2 January 1954 by the President of India. It is India's highest civilian honor.
  • Normal Annual Cap: Usually restricted to a maximum of 3 awards per year.
  • Exceptions to the Cap (Most Given at One Time):
    ▫ 1999: 4 awards given at a time (Jayaprakash Narayan, Amartya Sen, Gopinath Bordoloi, Ravi Shankar).
    ▫ 2024: 5 awards given in a single year (Karpoori Thakur, L.K. Advani, P.V. Narasimha Rao, Charan Singh, M.S. Swaminathan). This is the highest number ever announced at one time.
  • Total Recipients: 53 individuals conferred till date.

2. Record Holders & Business Leaders

  • Youngest Recipient: Sachin Tendulkar. Awarded in 2014 at the age of 40 (also the first and only sportsperson).
  • Oldest Recipient: Dhondo Keshav Karve. Awarded in 1958 on his 100th birthday.
    (L.K. Advani received it while living at age 96 in 2024).
  • Business Leaders / Industrialists:
    ▫ J.R.D. Tata (1992): The only industrialist and corporate leader to receive the Bharat Ratna. He founded Tata Airlines (Air India) and built modern Indian industry.
  • Foreign Non-Citizen Recipients: Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan (Pakistan, 1987) and Nelson Mandela (South Africa, 1990).

V. Exam-Trick Boxes (Avoid Common Traps!)
  • TRAP 1: "Bharat Ratna can be used as a prefix or suffix to the recipient's name." ➔ WRONG! In Balaji Raghavan v. UOI (1996), the Supreme Court ruled that National Awards are not titles under Article 18(1), but they cannot be used as prefixes or suffixes on letterheads, invitation cards, or publications.
  • TRAP 2: "Only Indian citizens can receive the Bharat Ratna." ➔ WRONG! There is no written rule restricting the award to Indian citizens. Two foreign nationals have received it: Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan (1987) and Nelson Mandela (1990). Mother Teresa was a naturalized citizen.
  • TRAP 3: "Posthumous awards were part of the original 1954 statutes." ➔ WRONG! The 1954 statutes had no provision for posthumous awards. The rules were amended in January 1966 to honor Lal Bahadur Shastri as the first posthumous awardee.
  • TRAP 4: "More than one business leader has received the Bharat Ratna." ➔ WRONG! J.R.D. Tata (1992) is the only industrialist to receive the Bharat Ratna.
VI. Special Note: Padma Awards Statutory Limits

📌 Annual Limits on Padma Awards (Padma Vibhushan, Padma Bhushan, Padma Shri)

• Total Annual Cap: The total number of Padma Awards given in any single year cannot exceed 120.
• Important Exclusions (Do Not Count in the 120 Limit):
  1. Posthumous awards are completely excluded from this 120 limit.
  2. Awards given to foreigners, Non-Resident Indians (NRIs), and Overseas Citizens of India (OCIs) are also excluded from the 120 limit.
• Duo Award Rule: In the case of a duo award (presented to two people together), the award is counted as one single award for the purpose of the 120 limit.

VIII. Mains Model Framework
Mains Practice • 10 Marks Q. "National civilian honors recognize exceptional public contribution while remaining distinct from pre-independence titles." Examine this statement with reference to constitutional limits and the convention governing Bharat Ratna and Padma awards.
View Model Answer Structuring Framework
Answer Framework:
• Introduction: State that national civilian honors (Bharat Ratna, Padma awards) were created in 1954 to recognize merit and public service in a democratic republic.
• Constitutional Distinction under Article 18(1):
▫ Pre-independence titles (Rai Bahadur, Sir, Maharaja) created aristocratic hierarchies, which Article 18 abolished.
▫ In Balaji Raghavan v. UOI (1996), the Supreme Court confirmed that these awards are state decorations recognizing merit, not titles of nobility.
▫ To preserve equality, awardees cannot use these awards as prefixes or suffixes to their names.
• Conventions and Statutory Limits:
▫ Bharat Ratna: Conventionally limited to 3 per year, though exceptional years like 1999 (4) and 2024 (5) expanded this to honor national stalwarts across fields.
▫ Padma Awards: Capped at 120 per year to maintain exclusivity, excluding posthumous awards and awards to foreigners.
• Conclusion: Civilian awards balance democratic equality under Article 14 with state recognition of outstanding service to the nation.
