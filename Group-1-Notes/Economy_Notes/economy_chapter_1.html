I. Inconsistent Economic Growth Trajectory

1. Growth Trends Across Eras

  • Pre-LPG Era (1947–1991): Averaged ~3.5% growth[cite: 10]. High state control, public sector monopoly, and import substitution caused low output[cite: 10].
  • LPG Reforms Era (1991–2019): Growth accelerated to 6%–10% bracket[cite: 10]. India became one of the fastest-growing major economies[cite: 10].
  • COVID-19 Shock & Bounce (2019–2023): Slumped to ~4% (2019-20), then crashed by -7.3% in 2020 (pandemic)[cite: 10]. Rebounded to 6%–7% via government capex and exports[cite: 10].
  • Present Outlook: Sustaining 6.0%–6.5% run rate[cite: 10]. Main engines: Digitalization, PLI push, and Gati Shakti[cite: 10].

2. Causes & Consequences of Inconsistency

  • Why Growth Fluctuate: Frequent policy changes[cite: 10], monsoon risks[cite: 10], lack of logistics[cite: 10], large informal workforce[cite: 10], and global price spikes[cite: 10].
  • Damaging Outcomes:
    ▫ Job Uncertainty: Unemployment hovers around 5.5%–6.5%[cite: 10]; high rural hidden underemployment[cite: 10].
    ▫ Extreme Wealth Divide: Top 10% hold 76%–78% of national wealth; bottom 50% get only 4%–6%[cite: 10].
    ▫ Fiscal Deficit Pressure: Tax drop during slowdowns pushes deficit up (peaked at 9.3% in FY21, now 5.1%–5.6%)[cite: 10].
📌 Memory Catch: Long-term steady policy + big public spending on roads/rail (Gati Shakti) is key to breaking erratic growth cycles[cite: 10].

III. Price Spikes, Oil Dependency & External Trade

1. Inflation & The Oil Price Nexus

  • Oil Import Trap: India imports >85% of crude oil[cite: 10]. Price swings ($40 in 2020 $\rightarrow$ $120+ in 2022 $\rightarrow$ $70–$90 in 2024–26) hit fuel directly[cite: 10].
  • Cost Pass-Through: Fuel hike raises freight and transport charges, driving up everyday food prices[cite: 10].
  • CPI Dynamics: Target is 4% (tolerance band of 2%–6%)[cite: 10]. Peaked at 6.3% in May 2022 before cooling to 4.5%–6.0%[cite: 10].
  • Key Shield: Underground Strategic Petroleum Reserves (SPR) and promoting domestic oil palm to cut imports[cite: 10].

2. Rupee Slide & Balance of Payments (BoP)

  • Current Account Deficit (CAD): Runs when merchandise imports (oil, gold, electronics) heavily outstrip exports[cite: 10]. Averaging 0.5%–2.0% of GDP[cite: 10].
  • Falling Rupee Value:
    ▫ Why it drops: Big trade gap[cite: 10], costly oil[cite: 10], and sudden exit of foreign portfolio capital[cite: 10].
    ▫ Impact: Makes imports pricier (imported inflation)[cite: 10] and increases the burden of paying back foreign loans[cite: 10].
  • Capital Inflows as Cushion: Stable FDI ($70–$85 billion/year) and remittances help offset trade gaps[cite: 10].

Balance of Payments (BoP) Blueprint

Current Account (Trade & Incomes):
  • Trade Balance (Visible): Physical goods (Crude oil, cars, computers vs gems, pharma)[cite: 10]. Usually in deficit[cite: 10].
  • Services Balance (Invisible): IT, software exports, consulting, tourism[cite: 10]. Usually in surplus.
  • Income Balance: Cross-border dividends, interest, corporate profits[cite: 10].
  • Transfers: Family remittances, gifts, foreign grants[cite: 10].
Capital & Financial Account:
  • Foreign Direct Investment (FDI): Long-term physical business setups[cite: 10].
  • Foreign Institutional Investment (FII/FPI): Short-term stock and bond trading[cite: 10].
  • Loans & Bank Accounts: External Commercial Borrowings (ECBs), NRI deposits, Government aid[cite: 10].
  • Forex Reserve Changes: Additions or drawdown by RBI to steady the rupee[cite: 10].
IV. Banking NPAs & Financial Cleanup

1. The NPA Wave & Its Fallout

  • What is an NPA: Loan overdue for 90 days or more without interest or principal payment[cite: 10].
  • Peak Crisis: Gross NPAs crossed 11% in FY18 due to stalled steel, power, and highway projects after 2011[cite: 10].
  • Consequences: Choked fresh credit flow[cite: 10], drained bank profits[cite: 10], and slowed economic investment[cite: 10].
  • Recovery Status: Fallen sharply to multi-year lows of 1.8%–2.5% due to cleaning bad debts and strict recognition[cite: 10].

2. Cleanup & Resolution Toolkit

  • IBC, 2016: Time-bound insolvency resolution[cite: 10]; recovered over ₹4 lakh crore and took control from defaulting owners[cite: 10].
  • SARFAESI Act, 2002: Allows banks to take over and auction securities without waiting for court nod[cite: 10].
  • NARCL (Bad Bank): Bundles and sells legacy toxic loans[cite: 10].
  • Recapitalization: Central government infused over ₹2.1 lakh crore into public sector banks to rebuild capital safety nets[cite: 10].

VI. Capital Scarcity, Inclusive Growth & Sustainability

1. Capital Deficit & Fiscal Strain

  • Resource Shortage: Low tax-to-GDP ratio restricts spending on healthcare, schools, and roads[cite: 10].
  • Public Debt Levels: General government debt peaked at 89.2% of GDP in 2020 and eased to ~81.4%[cite: 10].
  • Government Interventions:
    ▫ National Infrastructure Pipeline (NIP): Forward map for infrastructure investments[cite: 10].
    ▫ Asset Monetization: Leasing operational roads and airports to raise funds[cite: 10].
    ▫ Jan Dhan Yojana: Mobilizing small domestic savings into formal banking[cite: 10].

2. Lack of Inclusive & Green Growth

  • Wealth Concentration: Richest 1% holds 40.1% of national wealth, showing persistent inequality[cite: 10].
  • Ecological Stress: India placed 176th out of 177 on the Environmental Performance Index (EPI)[cite: 10].
  • Balancing Welfare & Green Power:
    ▫ Welfare: MGNREGA safety net[cite: 10], PMAY housing[cite: 10], Ayushman Bharat healthcare[cite: 10].
    ▫ Green Push: Scaling up solar and wind capacity to decouple growth from emissions[cite: 10].

IX. Exam-Trick Boxes (Avoid Common Traps!)
  • TRAP 1: "Balance of Trade (BOT) covers both merchandise goods and IT services." ➔ WRONG! BOT accounts only for physical visible goods[cite: 10]. Services (IT, travel, consulting) fall strictly under the Invisibles account of the Current Account[cite: 10].
  • TRAP 2: "Fugitive Economic Offenders Act (FEOA) applies to all economic defaults." ➔ WRONG! FEOA applies strictly to economic frauds where the total value involved is ₹100 crore or more[cite: 10].
  • TRAP 3: "A loan is marked as an NPA immediately after a single missed EMI." ➔ WRONG! An asset is formally classified as an NPA only after overdue default on interest or principal continues for 90 days or more[cite: 10].
  • TRAP 4: "Black money and money laundering are identical offences under law." ➔ WRONG! Black money is untaxed undisclosed income (tax compliance issue)[cite: 10]. Money laundering is the systematic conversion of crime proceeds into clean money (criminal offence under PMLA)[cite: 10].
  • TRAP 5: "FDI is volatile hot-money while FPI is long-term investment." ➔ WRONG! FDI represents long-term physical business investments[cite: 10]. FPI is liquid, short-term portfolio money that can exit financial markets rapidly during shocks[cite: 10].

Mains Practice • 10 Marks Q1. "Despite robust GDP growth, India faces a dual structural challenge: an overburdened agricultural sector and an underperforming manufacturing base." Analyze the causes and outline policy remedies.
View Model Answer Structuring Framework
Answer Framework:
• Introduction: India presents a structural disconnect where agriculture contributes only ~16% to GDP but sustains ~46.1% of the workforce[cite: 10], while manufacturing contributes 17% of GVA and employs only 11%[cite: 10].
• Causes of Slow Agriculture Growth:
▫ Land Fragmentation: Small, uneconomic holdings limit farm mechanization[cite: 10].
▫ Monsoon Dependence: Erratic weather causes high crop failure risks[cite: 10].
▫ Low Capital Investment: Private corporate capital formation in farming remains below 2%[cite: 10].
• Causes of Manufacturing Bottlenecks:
▫ Infrastructure & Logistics: High power tariffs and transport costs undermine price competitiveness[cite: 10].
▫ Regulatory Friction: Complex labor laws and compliance burdens discourage business scale[cite: 10].
▫ MSME Dominance: 95% of units are unorganized with limited R&D access[cite: 10].
• Policy Interventions:
▫ Agri: Micro-irrigation under PMKSY[cite: 10], e-NAM market unification[cite: 10], and cold chains via PM Kisan Sampada[cite: 10].
▫ Manufacturing: PLI schemes across 14 sectors[cite: 10], Gati Shakti connectivity[cite: 10], and single-window industrial clearances[cite: 10].
• Conclusion: Bridging the gap requires shifting surplus farm labor into high-value manufacturing and agro-processing to achieve the 2047 target of 27% manufacturing GVA[cite: 10].

Mains Practice • 10 Marks Q3. "Evaluate the structural impact of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC) in resolving the NPA crisis in the Indian banking system."
View Model Answer Structuring Framework
Answer Framework:
• Introduction: Severe corporate stress caused Gross NPAs to peak at over 11% in FY18[cite: 10]. The IBC (2016) introduced a systemic shift toward time-bound, market-led asset resolution[cite: 10].
• Structural Contributions of IBC:
▫ Creditor Control: Replaced promoter-in-possession with creditor-in-control, ending wilful default leverage[cite: 10].
▫ Resolution & Recovery: Facilitated recoveries exceeding ₹4 lakh crore, strengthening bank balance sheets[cite: 10].
▫ Credit Discipline: The prospect of losing corporate ownership spurred operational repayments prior to formal admission.
• Supporting Reforms: Bank recapitalization of over ₹2.1 lakh crore[cite: 10], NARCL establishment for toxic asset offloading[cite: 10], and strict RBI AQR reviews[cite: 10].
• Outcome: Gross NPAs declined to a multi-year low of 1.8%–2.5%, rebuilding commercial lending capacity[cite: 10].
• Conclusion: Strengthening judicial infrastructure and reducing tribunal backlogs is vital to sustain time-bound debt resolution.
