Chapter 3: Resource Mobilization in Andhra Pradesh

Budgetary Resources & Constraints • AP Bifurcation Act (2014) • Debt & External Projects • Minerals, Forests & Water Conflicts

Syllabus Scope & Core Themes

  • Core Concepts: Resource mobilization covers gathering money to run state welfare and growth projects.
  • Key Syllabus Points: State budget sources and fiscal limits.; fulfillment of AP Bifurcation Act (2014).; Central grants and state conflicts.; public debt and Externally Aided Projects (EAPs).; physical resources like minerals and forests.; and river water disputes with neighboring states (Krishna, Godavari, Polavaram, Telugu Ganga).
  • Root Cause of State Fiscal Stress: High interest payments on debt., big welfare spending commitments., high staff salaries., and the loss of revenue-rich Hyderabad city.
I. Budgetary Resources & Constraints in Andhra Pradesh

1. Sources of Budgetary Money in AP

  • Tax Revenues: Money collected via State GST, sales tax (fuel, alcohol), stamp duty, land registration, vehicle tax, and excise duty.
  • Central Grants: Funds given by the Central government for specific development projects and welfare schemes.
  • Borrowings: Taking loans from financial institutions and issuing market bonds to fund big infrastructure works.
  • State-Owned Enterprises (PSUs): Dividends and profits earned from public-sector units and state companies.
  • Natural Resources: Mining royalties, farm produce, and forest sales.
  • Public-Private Partnerships (PPPs): Getting private companies to invest in building public roads and energy projects.

2. Main Budgetary Constraints (Limits)

  • Loss of Hyderabad Hub: Losing Hyderabad took away the state's biggest commercial, tax, and IT money generator.
  • Heavy Debt Burden: High debt means paying large interest amounts, which leaves less money for public services.
  • Welfare & Salary Demands: Huge ongoing spending on social welfare programs, pensions, and staff salaries strains the treasury.
  • Fiscal Deficits: Spending more than revenue causes high fiscal deficits.
  • Economic Downturns: Slumps in trade hurt tax collections.
  • Infrastructure Needs: Massive money is required to build a new greenfield capital, ports, and power plants.
  • Administrative Hurdles: Checking tax evasion and reducing leakages needs strong governance systems.
  • Natural & Weather Risks: Cyclone damages along the coast and drought in Rayalaseema divert funds.

III. AP Bifurcation Act (2014): Promises & Issues of Conflict

1. Promised Central Assistance

  • Revenue Deficit Grant: The 2014 Act mandated the Centre to give revenue deficit grants to cover the sudden loss of revenue-generating areas to Telangana. But funds were often delayed; for example, in FY 2019-20, AP received only ₹2,500 crore against a much larger demand.
  • Special Development Package: The Act promised financial packages to develop backward districts in Rayalaseema and North Coastal Andhra. However, slow monitoring and project delays slowed results.

2. Ongoing Points of Conflict

  • Division of Assets & Liabilities: Sharing government buildings, state corporations, and training centers located in Hyderabad remains a point of dispute.
  • Apportionment of Public Debt: Disputes remain over who should repay historical loans taken for irrigation and infrastructure.
  • Division of Government Employees: Shifting and allocating state workers caused personnel disputes and administrative delays.
  • Capital City Problem: Building the new capital Amaravati faced disagreements over plans, decentralization ideas, and funding between the state and central government.

V. Physical Resources: Minerals & Forests in AP

1. Major Mineral Wealth

  • Bauxite: Abundant in the Eastern Ghats; Andhra Pradesh produces about 7% of India's total bauxite.
  • Limestone: Plentiful in Guntur, Krishna, and Kurnool; forms the raw material backbone for cement factories.
  • Gold: Gold deposits are located along the Kurnool-Guntur-Cuddapah-Anantapur belt, drawing mining investments.
  • Mica: Concentrated in large amounts in the Nellore district, contributing heavily to national mica supply.
  • Granite: High-quality commercial granite is quarried in Prakasam, Chittoor, and Kurnool for local and overseas markets.

2. Forest Resources & Green Targets

  • Forest Area: Recorded forest cover occupies 23.4% of total state land.
  • Red Sandalwood: Precious red sandalwood (Pterocarpus santalinus) grows in the Seshachalam Hills of the Eastern Ghats and is heavily protected from illegal trade.
  • Mangroves: Coastal mangrove forests flourish in the Godavari and Krishna river deltas, shielding shorelines from tidal erosion.
  • Wildlife: Houses rich animal species, including the large Nagarjunasagar-Srisailam Tiger Reserve.
  • Total Green Baseline: Broader green cover baseline (forests + trees outside forests) stands at 30%.
  • Mission Haritha Andhra Pradesh Targets: Sets official goals to reach 37% green cover by 2030 and 50% total cover by 2047.
VI. Inter-State River Water Disputes
Dispute Area Tribunal Awards & Historical Shares Active Conflicts, Court Petitions & Ratios
1. Krishna River Dispute • Bachawat Tribunal (1976): Awarded 811 TMC of Krishna water to undivided Andhra Pradesh.
• Regulated by the Interstate River Water Disputes Act, 1956.		 • Interim Share: Working sharing ratio stands at 66:34 (AP : Telangana).
• Active Conflicts: In January 2025, AP rejected Telangana's call for a 50:50 equal split. In early 2026 hearings, AP challenged Telangana's claim that Amaravati's capital city modernization should lower AP's farm water share.
2. Godavari River Dispute • GWDT Award (1980): Allocated 949 TMC of Godavari water to undivided Andhra Pradesh. • Polavaram-Banakacherla Friction: Telangana filed a Supreme Court petition opposing AP's tenders for the Polavaram-Banakacherla Link Project (PBLP), attempting to halt central clearances and funds.
3. Polavaram Project Controversy • Large multi-purpose dam on the Godavari river.; declared a National Project under the AP Bifurcation Act.
• Requires clearances from CWC, Environment Ministry (MoEFCC), and the Polavaram Project Authority.		 • Project Cost: Total estimated cost exceeds ₹62,000 crore.; AP's 2026-27 Budget allocated ₹6,105 crore with matching Central support.
• Interstate Protests: Odisha and Chhattisgarh challenge submergence and rehabilitation issues.
• Supreme Court Action: On July 13, 2026, the Supreme Court refused Telangana's plea to freeze work on the 200 TMC flood lift project, telling states to resolve technical points through central bodies.
4. Telugu Ganga Project Dispute • Canal taking flood water from Srisailam reservoir to provide drinking water to Chennai and irrigate Rayalaseema.
• 15 TMC Sharing: Maharashtra, Karnataka, and undivided AP each agreed to give 5 TMC (total 15 TMC at source, giving a net 12 TMC at the Tamil Nadu border after transmission losses).		 • New Basin Conflict: Telangana challenged AP before KWDT-II, arguing that diverting Krishna water outside the river basin hurts irrigation within the basin.
• Operational Changes: AP resumed water via the KP Canal in May 2026; poor rains dropped flow to 188 cusecs in July 2026, prompting Tamil Nadu delegations to visit AP for emergency water releases.

VIII. Exam-Trick Boxes (Avoid Common Mistakes!)
  • TRAP 1: "Krishna river water is shared equally (50:50) between AP and Telangana." ➔ WRONG! The official working arrangement is 66:34 (AP : Telangana). In early 2025, AP rejected Telangana's demand for equal 50:50 sharing.
  • TRAP 2: "The Telugu Ganga Project was built to irrigate farmland in Tamil Nadu." ➔ WRONG! It was built exclusively to provide drinking water to Chennai city (supplying a net 12 TMC after transmission losses). Irrigation benefits are limited to Andhra Pradesh.
  • TRAP 3: "All public debt from undivided Andhra Pradesh was divided based only on population." ➔ WRONG! While general debts were shared by population ratio, debts taken for specific irrigation and infrastructure projects were divided based on project location., which caused continuing disputes.
  • TRAP 4: "Mission Haritha Andhra Pradesh aims for 30% green cover by 2047." ➔ WRONG! 30% is the current baseline. The official targets are 37% by 2030 and 50% by 2047.
  • TRAP 5: "The Polavaram project is completely funded by the State of Andhra Pradesh." ➔ WRONG! Polavaram is a National Project under the 2014 Act. The Central Government reimburses the construction costs through the Polavaram Project Authority.
IX. Latest Current Affairs & Developments (2024–2026)

1. Amaravati Capital Funding Approvals

  • $1.6 Billion Multilateral Package: World Bank and Asian Development Bank (ADB) committed $800 million each (total $1,600 million / ~₹13,600 crore) for Phase-I development of Amaravati capital city.
  • Central Support: Central government committed ₹15,000 crore through multilateral facilitation.
  • Disbursement Status: World Bank released its first tranche of $207 million in March 2025, with a second installment of $200 million scheduled for disbursement, backed by monthly reviews.

2. Polavaram Dam & Krishna Basin Disputes

  • Union Budget Central Allocations: The Central Government allocated ₹5,936 crore in the 2025-26 Union Budget to expedite Polavaram project construction, with water storage permitted up to 41.15 meters.
  • Polavaram-Banakacherla Link (PBLP): The Supreme Court declined Telangana's stay petition against AP's 200 TMC flood lift link, directing technical issues to the Godavari River Management Board.
  • 66:34 Krishna Formula Extension: Andhra Pradesh proposed extending the 66:34 water-sharing ratio until July 2027, whereas Telangana continues to seek a 50:50 allocation.

XI. APPSC & UPSC Mains Model Frameworks
Mains Practice • 10 Marks Q1. "Examine the root causes behind the fiscal stress in post-bifurcation Andhra Pradesh. How have rising debt and bifurcation constraints affected the state's budget?"
View Model Answer Structuring Framework
Answer Framework:
• Introduction: The 2014 bifurcation separated Andhra Pradesh from Hyderabad, removing its main commercial tax revenue base while leaving it with heavy liabilities.
• Causes of Fiscal Stress:
▫ Loss of Hyderabad: Corporate tax and IT revenues stayed with Telangana, creating an immediate revenue deficit.
▫ High Debt Burden: State public debt is projected at ₹7,11,094 crore (36% of GSDP) in 2026-27.; total debt including PSUs stands at ₹9,74,556 crore.
▫ High Committed Costs: Heavy spending on salaries, pensions, and welfare schemes leaves less money for building assets.
▫ Central Grant Delays: Disagreements and delays over the amount of Revenue Deficit Grants and Special Packages.
• Comparison with All-India Stats: India's fiscal deficit is around 5.1%–5.6%[cite: 10], while AP targets a 3.8% fiscal deficit (₹75,868 crore) and a 1.1% revenue deficit (₹22,003 crore).
• Solutions: Broaden state GST revenues., complete industrial corridors (VCIC) with ADB funding[cite: 11], and secure multilateral loans for Amaravati development.
• Conclusion: Prudent debt management and capital creation are vital to stabilize Andhra Pradesh's finances.
